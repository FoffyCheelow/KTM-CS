Build a working web app called **Community Studio Space Planner** for the Community Studio at Kentish Town Meanwhile, London.

The app should help organisers request space and help studio managers coordinate simultaneous activities. Its main feature must be an interactive floor plan that allocates **whole, adjacent workspace zones**, taking existing bookings into account.

Use these reference PDFs:
- Community Studio Use and Spatial Plan.pdf
- 261607_Kentish Town Meanwhile_First Floor Plan.pdf

Read both before implementation. Treat the spatial plan and occupancy analysis as preliminary planning material rather than an approved fire strategy.

**1. Space model and provisional areas**

The studio is approximately 3,600 sq ft / 334.5 m².

Model:
- Ten flexible workspace zones, numbered 1–10, covering a combined estimated 2,400 sq ft / 222.97 m².
- A separate fixed dirty workshop, provisionally approximately 900 sq ft / 83.6 m².
- Any remaining space as unallocated/non-bookable until its purpose and boundaries are confirmed. Do not automatically distribute it among the zones.

Support four activity types:
- Dirty workshop.
- Clean workshop.
- Exhibition.
- Meeting.

Clean workshop, exhibition and meeting activities share Zones 1–10. Zones are not permanently assigned to one activity type.

Keep the dirty workshop fixed and reserved even when unoccupied. It is not Zone 11 and must never be included in flexible-zone allocations.

**3. Whole-zone allocation rules**

The smallest flexible-space allocation is **one complete zone**. The largest is **all ten zones**.

Never:
- Allocate a fraction of a zone.
- Subdivide a zone between simultaneous bookings.
- Allocate disconnected zones to one activity.
- Count an unbooked zone as a connection through another activity’s footprint.

For automatic allocation:
1. Identify zones available throughout the requested interval.
2. Generate connected combinations of available zones.
3. Calculate each combination’s area and applicable constraints.
4. Select the smallest sufficient number of zones.
5. Among equally suitable options, favour configured preferences, compact shapes and less unused area.

Because there are only ten zones, evaluate all possible non-empty zone combinations where practical rather than relying on an opaque placement heuristic.

Support editable preferred allocations:
- Clean workshop for 20 people: prefer Zones 4, 5, 9 and 10.
- Meeting for five people: prefer Zone 7.

Preferences must not override existing bookings, area checks or approved limits.

For the 20-person clean-workshop example:
- Display Zones 4, 5, 9 and 10 as the preferred proposal.
- Their provisional combined area is 960 sq ft / approximately 89.19 m².
- If the configured workshop rule requires 100 m², show the area shortfall explicitly.
- Suggest adding an available adjoining zone, such as Zone 3 or Zone 8, and require acceptance before changing the proposal.
- Do not silently reduce the area requirement or present the four-zone proposal as meeting that rule.

For a five-person meeting:
- Allocate the whole of Zone 7 when available and suitable.
- If unavailable, suggest another suitable single zone before considering a larger allocation.

Let managers manually select zones, with the same connectivity, availability and capacity validation.

**4. Interactive floor plan**

Create a scalable 2D plan using SVG or an equivalent interactive approach.

Trace the actual architectural footprint, walls, columns, openings and obstructions. Overlay the ten zones in their positions from the spatial plan. Do not replace the drawing with a generic rectangular room.

Show:
- Permanent zone boundaries and numbers.
- Fixed dirty workshop.
- Available, booked, proposed and unavailable zones.
- Existing activities at the selected date/time.
- Protected circulation and exit locations.
- Activity labels, headcounts and allocated areas.

Selecting an activity should highlight its full group of zones. Selecting a zone should reveal its area, availability and booking details.

Include zoom, pan, a legend, a linked booking list and accessible labels/patterns alongside colour.

Provide administrator controls for scale calibration, zone boundaries, areas, adjacency, obstructions and protected routes. Changing visual boundaries must not silently overwrite verified or provisional area values.

Whole-zone booking never authorises blocking protected routes within that zone.

**5. Activity request form and scheduling**

Collect:
- Activity name and course/organisation.
- Activity type and layout subtype.
- Participants and additional staff, tutors, technicians or visitors.
- Start/end dates and times.
- Setup and pack-down periods.
- Equipment/furniture requirements.
- Accessibility and spatial requirements.
- Optional preferred zones, minimum area and notes.

Use Europe/London time, including daylight-saving changes.

Count everyone present in occupancy. Track physical space reservations separately from people present so installations can reserve zones overnight.

Keep the same zone group reserved throughout an activity, including setup and pack-down. Do not silently move activities between zones.

Check every interval created by relevant booking boundaries for:
- Zone conflicts.
- Shared equipment conflicts.
- Simultaneous occupancy limits.
- Operational incompatibilities.

Preserve confirmed bookings. Any proposed rearrangement must be shown explicitly and accepted by a manager.

**6. Area and occupancy rules**

Maintain three separate concepts:
1. Floor-space-factor occupancy estimate.
2. Practical layout requirements.
3. Approved operational limits.

Consult current applicable official guidance for England, including Approved Document B Volume 2, the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 and relevant fire-risk-assessment guidance. Use any site-specific fire strategy and assessment supplied.

Record source links, editions, sections, effective dates and verification status. Do not apply forthcoming amendments automatically.

Initial reference factors to verify:
- Workshop: 5.0 m²/person.
- Meeting/conference room: 1.0 m²/person.
- Exhibition hall: 1.5 m²/person.
- Art gallery: 5.0 m²/person.

Require administrator confirmation of the appropriate classification. Do not automatically apply high-density assembly factors to meetings or performances.

Show:
- Total people.
- Selected classification and factor.
- Indicative area requirement.
- Practical layout requirement.
- Selected zones and their combined area.
- Any shortfall or spare area.
- Applicable approved limits and unresolved checks.

Do not independently round each zone’s capacity and then add those rounded numbers. For a combined activity, calculate from its combined applicable area, then round the resulting planning headcount down.

Keep regulatory area-measurement rules distinct from practical free-floor-area calculations. Avoid double-counting furniture and circulation allowances.

Support approved limits for individual zones, zone combinations, the whole studio, shared escape routes, equipment and operational constraints.

Missing limits must remain unverified. Allow draft planning, but prevent confirmation where required information is missing or a configured blocking constraint fails. Never describe an area-based result as proof of fire compliance.

**7. Interface and workflow**

Provide:
- Date selector and daily timeline.
- Large interactive floor plan.
- Request form beside the plan.
- Summary showing selected zone numbers, zone count, allocated area and occupancy.
- Plain-language conflict explanations.
- Expandable calculations and assumptions.
- Printable allocation summary.

Clearly distinguish:
- Fits configured planning constraints.
- Requires review.
- Cannot accommodate as requested.

Support draft, pending, confirmed and cancelled bookings. Drafts should not block availability by default; make reservation behaviour explicit for every status.

Managers can configure rules and confirm bookings. Ordinary users can request space and view availability.

When a request cannot be accommodated, suggest validated alternatives: another connected zone group, another time, fewer occupants or a different layout.

**8. Implementation and verification**

Use the existing project stack if present; otherwise choose a maintainable TypeScript web stack.

Separate zone geometry, adjacency, allocation, scheduling and occupancy calculations from interface code. Use deterministic allocation logic.

Persist bookings, selected zone IDs, area assumptions, rule versions and configuration. Keep an audit trail and revalidate affected future bookings when rules or geometry change.

For shared use, validate on the server and prevent simultaneous confirmations from double-booking zones or exceeding shared capacity.

Include labelled demonstration bookings and focused tests proving that:
- The ten flexible zones total 2,400 sq ft.
- Zone numbering and adjacency match the supplied plan.
- The minimum allocation is one zone and maximum is ten.
- Disconnected selections are rejected.
- A five-person meeting prefers Zone 7.
- A 20-person clean workshop proposes Zones 4, 5, 9 and 10 and flags any configured area shortfall.
- A connected additional zone is suggested where necessary.
- Unavailable preferred zones trigger valid alternatives.
- Setup, pack-down and overnight reservations block the relevant zones.
- Staff and simultaneous occupants are counted.
- The dirty workshop is excluded from flexible allocation.
- Saved bookings survive reload.
- Concurrent confirmation cannot double-book zones.

Build and verify the working app. Provide setup instructions and identify the information needed to replace provisional assumptions before operational use.
